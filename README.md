## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
Create a digital marketplace that connects farmers and Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs) with households, retailers, restaurants, and wholesalers. Farmers can list available produce, quantities, prices, and harvest dates. Buyers can browse listings, compare offers, and place orders directly.

The platform will also coordinate produce pickup and delivery. AI-based demand forecasting can help farmers and FPOs estimate what buyers are likely to need, while route optimization can help delivery partners plan efficient trips. Starting with a local pilot and expanding to nearby regions can help build trust and improve the service over time.

## Technical Approach
The solution can be developed as a mobile-friendly web application, with the option to add Android and iOS apps as the marketplace grows. It will have three main user areas:
- Farmer/FPO: Create listings, update availability and prices, and manage orders.
- Buyer: Search and compare produce, place orders, and track delivery.
- Logistics partner: Accept delivery requests, coordinate pickups, and view suggested routes.
A backend service will manage user accounts, listings, orders, payments, and delivery updates. A database will store product, transaction, and delivery information. Demand forecasting can use past orders, seasonal patterns, and local market trends. Route optimization can use delivery locations and order details to suggest efficient routes.

## Feasibility and Viability
The platform can be launched in one region with a small group of FPOs, buyers, and delivery partners, then expanded as participation grows. Key challenges include limited digital access, inconsistent produce quality, and coordinating timely deliveries. These can be addressed through local onboarding support, clear quality standards, and partnerships with existing transport providers. Revenue could come from a modest transaction fee or logistics services, while keeping farmer pricing transparent.

## Impact and Benefits
Farmers and FPOs can access more buyers and gain greater control over pricing. Consumers and businesses can find produce with fewer intermediaries. Better demand estimates and delivery planning can reduce unsold produce, spoilage, transport costs, and delays. The platform can also support rural livelihoods by improving access to digital markets and logistics.

## Research and References
Research local farming and FPO networks, buyer demand, produce pricing, seasonal availability, and existing transport options before piloting. Potential sources include government agriculture and market data, FPOs, local cooperatives, buyers, and logistics providers.
